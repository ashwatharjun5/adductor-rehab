Phase 1 — Down-Regulation

Calm the nervous system, break involuntary muscle spindle firing via reciprocal inhibition

📅 Days 1–14
Objective: The adductors are in a sustained neurological guard state — this is a software problem, not structural failure. Goal is to down-regulate the threat signal via parasympathetic activation and low-load reciprocal inhibition, not to push through pain.
Supported 90/90 Diaphragmatic Breathing
Legs elevated on couch at 90°. 4-second nasal inhale, expanding pelvic floor outward. 8-second pursed-lip exhale, softening groin. Focus on full pelvic floor drop — not belly breathing.
BID (twice daily) 10 min per session Pain: 0/10
Isometric Active Abduction
Supine, knees bent, feet flat. Press knees outward against your own hands at 20% effort only. This activates glutes/abductors via reciprocal inhibition — firing the antagonist to quiet the adductor.
3 sets × 10 reps Hold 10 seconds each ≤20% effort
Prone Glute Max Isolation
Lie prone, knee bent to 90°. Hover knee 1 inch off the surface using pure glute squeeze — zero hamstring cramping allowed. If you feel hamstring engagement, ease off. This wakes up the posterior chain as a hip stabilizer.
2 sets × 10 reps 1s hold at top Glute-only focus
✅
Phase 1 Clearance Criteria VAS pain ≤ 2/10 at rest  |  Zero adductor spasms during normal indoor walking  |  Able to complete Phase 1 exercises without pain increase

Phase 2 — Static Stabilization

Teach the brain to stabilize the femoral head without using the adductor emergency brake

📅 Days 15–30
Objective: Build a reliable proprioceptive map so the CNS stops defaulting to the adductor as its emergency stabilizer. The hip joint needs to feel safe in space — gluteus medius must become the primary stabilizer before any dynamic loading.
Tripod Single-Leg Balance
Barefoot. Level ASIS bilaterally. Apply tripod foot (big toe joint + little toe joint + heel center). Micro-hinge hip 2–5°. Regress with index finger on counter if swaying — the point is zero groin recruitment.
3 sets × 45 seconds Both legs Zero groin tightness
Bilateral Glute Bridge with Abduction Band
Loop band around knees. Drive heels firmly into floor. Bridge up while simultaneously pressing knees apart against band resistance. Hold 2 seconds at top. The abduction force activates glute med while bridging activates glute max.
3 sets × 12 reps 2s hold at top Light–medium band
Tall Kneeling Pallof Press
Kneel tall, hips fully extended (no hip hinge), anchor resistance band at chest height to your side. Press band straight out from chest, hold 2s, return. Zero torso rotation permitted — this challenges anti-rotation stability under pelvic control.
3 sets × 10 reps Both sides 2s hold extended
✅
Phase 2 Clearance Criteria 60-second single-leg balance with zero groin pain  |  Can stand for 1 continuous hour (morning rounds simulation)  |  No adductor guarding on palpation

Phase 3 — Linear Loading

Reintroduce progressive mechanical load in a strict straight-line plane — post-shift only

📅 Weeks 5–8 (July–August)
Objective: Begin axial tendon loading post-residency shift. Exercises are linear plane only — zero lateral stress. This phase runs concurrently with intern duties; all sessions are post-shift, ≤20 minutes, low overhead.
Bodyweight Box Squats
Slow 3-second descent to a chair (glutes tap lightly — don't fully unload). Progressively widen stance across weeks: narrow → shoulder-width → slightly wide. Emphasize tripod foot and glute activation on the way up.
3 sets × 10 reps 3s descent tempo Week-by-week stance widening
Supported Linear Lunges
Hold a railing or wall with one hand to eliminate lateral stabilization demands. Step directly forward (no diagonal). Keep rear knee tracking straight. The support allows the hip to load without recruiting the adductor as a dynamic stabilizer.
3 sets × 8 reps Both legs Rail support required
Seated Adductor Isometric Squeezes
Sit in a chair, pillow between knees. Squeeze at strictly 10–20% effort — no more. Hold 5 seconds, full release. This introduces graded tendon loading — controlled enough to stimulate collagen remodeling without triggering the guarding reflex.
3 sets × 10 reps 5s hold, full release 10–20% effort MAX
✅
Phase 3 Clearance Criteria 50% effort pain-free adductor squeeze  |  Complete a 4-hour standing OR case using modifications (stool, wide stance)  |  No post-shift pain escalation next morning

Phase 4 — Eccentric Remodeling

Lengthen and strengthen remodeled scar tissue under tension; restore lateral deceleration capacity

📅 Weeks 9–12+ (Autumn)
Objective: Eccentric loading is the gold standard for tendinopathy remodeling. This phase lengthens tissue under load — driving fibroblast alignment along stress lines and replacing disorganized scar collagen with load-bearing Type I collagen. Slow tempos mandatory.
Side-Lying Adductor Lifts
Lie on injured side. Cross top leg over and place foot flat for support. Lift bottom straight leg toward ceiling 6–8 inches. Slow 4-second lowering phase is the eccentric stimulus. Do not let the leg drop.
3 sets × 10 reps 4s eccentric lowering Both sides
Eccentric Slider Lateral Lunges
Uninjured foot stable on floor. Injured foot on towel or furniture slider. Slide outward slowly (3–4 seconds) into a lateral lunge as far as comfortable. Pull back in using glute contraction — not a passive snap back. This eccentrically loads adductor in the lengthened position.
3 sets × 6 reps 3–4s slide out Glute-driven return
Copenhagen Plank Regression (Short Lever)
Lie on your side. Rest injured knee (not ankle) on a bench — this is the short lever regression. Push up so your body forms a straight line. Hold. This builds adductor endurance under bodyweight loading in an anti-gravity position. Progress to ankle-on-bench when ready.
3 sets × 15 seconds Knee on bench (short lever) Straight body line

Clinical Context & OR Playbook

The FAI Link

Post-FAI surgery, even with successful cam/pincer and labral repair, the hip's structural geometry has changed. The femoral head now tracks slightly differently in the acetabulum during gait and stance. The adductor longus — whose primary job is to pull the femoral head medially and assist in pelvic stability — picks up the stabilization workload that the labrum and bony geometry no longer fully provide. Over months, this overcurrent loading causes microtrauma, collagen disorganization, and the fibrotic thickening you're experiencing at the musculotendinous junction.

Tendinopathy vs. Acute Strain

Pure tendinopathy is degenerative, not inflammatory — this is why NSAIDs don't help (and actively impair healing). The pathological tissue is disorganized Type III collagen, neovascularization, and failed tenocyte remodeling. The acute bilateral "flare" you're experiencing is not a new tear — it is superimposed neural guarding on a chronic tendinopathic substrate, triggered by accumulated threat perception.

Why Shockwave Helped But Destabilized You

Shockwave (ESWT) is highly effective at stimulating tenocyte activity and breaking down calcific deposits — this is why you gained tendon strength. However, ESWT does not address the neuromuscular control deficit upstream of the tendon. Stronger tissue with worse motor patterning = more efficient loading of a still-poorly-stabilized joint. Hence the stability regression after treatment.

⚠️ NSAID Warning: Ibuprofen, Naproxen, and Meloxicam block the COX-2 pathway required for prostaglandin-driven tendon healing cascades. Use Acetaminophen only for acute soreness during training and floor-of-hospital duties.

Guarding Is a Software Problem, Not Hardware Failure

The bilateral spasm flare is not a structural failure — it is a neurological executive decision. Your brain has elevated the threat rating of your hip beyond a tolerable threshold and has commanded the adductors to perform an involuntary isometric contraction — essentially locking the femoral head into the socket as a temporary splint. This is the same mechanism seen in acute back pain and protective spasm.

The Threat Bucket Model

Central sensitization works via a cumulative threat load. Your current bucket contains: residency start anxiety + fatigue/sleep debt + chronic hip pathology + fear of re-injury during long OR cases. When this bucket overflows, the brain's pain volume knob turns up — even low-load movements trigger outsized neural responses. The goal is not to suppress pain, but to drain the threat bucket systematically.

Reciprocal Inhibition — The Neurological Override

The Phase 1 protocol (isometric abduction, glute activation) works by Sherrington's Law of Reciprocal Inhibition: activating the antagonist muscle (glute med/abductors) sends an inhibitory signal to the adductor motor neurons, temporarily quieting the guard reflex. This is not a stretch — it's a neural reset.

💡 Reframe for the brain: When you feel guarding onset, consciously label it: "My nervous system is protecting me — this is not a structural injury signal." This prefrontal override has measurable effects on pain neuroscience outcomes. Combine with a 90/90 breathing reset.

Pre-Case Communication Script

Use this exact framing before any case likely to exceed 90 minutes:

"Just a heads-up — I'm managing an adductor strain from a sports injury. To keep my pelvis stable and assist effectively, I'm going to grab a surgical stool for this case. Happy to discuss positioning with the team."

Intraoperative Positioning Modifications

Stance
Stand shoulder-width or slightly wider to distribute axial load across both hips — avoids unilateral hip impingement postures
Stool Coordination
Brief the circulating nurse to slide in a surgical stool before draping. Use it proactively — not reactively when in pain
Retraction Force
Shift retraction force to upper body (shoulders, forearms). Avoid leaning backward or loading the posterior chain when pulling
Micro-Break Protocol
On any natural pause (instrument exchange, waiting for specimen), perform a 10-second standing isometric glute squeeze to reset proprioception

Post-Shift Recovery Protocol

Immediately post-shift (within 30 minutes): 10 minutes of 90/90 breathing. Then Phase 1 or 2 exercises depending on current protocol week. No Phase 3/4 exercises on days with 8+ hour OR duties — recovery is the training stimulus on those days.

⚠️ Red Line: If bilateral guarding returns mid-shift, do not push through. Step out, perform 5 minutes of standing 90/90 breathing at the scrub sink. Inform your senior: "I need 5 minutes — hip is flaring." This is a medical issue. You have disclosed it. Act accordingly.
